The Slow Web

The web used to be a place where people wrote long pieces and other people read them carefully. Over the last two decades, feeds replaced pages, and attention fragmented into thousands of tiny pieces that few readers ever finish.

Saving an article for later is an act of intent. It says that a piece deserves real time, away from notifications, and that the reader will come back when they can give it that time. A good reading tool respects that intent by removing clutter and keeping only the words.

What a reader needs

A reader needs a clean typographic page, a predictable layout, and the confidence that the copy they saved will still exist next month even if the original site disappears or rewrites the story without notice.

  • Readable text without advertising
  • Links that still work, opening safely
  • A lead image for context

A relative link and an absolute link should both survive.
